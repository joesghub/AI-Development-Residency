## What the Grid Shows

- **No competitor offers Feature 5.** Scheduling wants with automatic Buy Days is unique to Patiencents, which makes it the center of the pitch.
- **Only Rule offers Feature 6,** and it's UK-based and early-stage. In the U.S., no competitor connects daily restraint to bank-confirmed progress.
- **No competitor combines Features 4, 5, and 6.** That combination is the Patiencents loop: a daily number, leftovers that fill the Patience Pot, and the Pot funding Buy Days.
- **YNAB** remains strongest on ADHD education but fails on effort, daily focus, and restart support.
- **PocketGuard** is low-effort but offers no motivation or planning for wants.
- **Patiencents' one 🚫,** ADHD-specific education, is a deliberate MVP trade-off, not a gap in the vision.

## Notes on Borderline Ratings

- **YNAB, Feature 4:** changed from ✅ in v1 to 🚫. Its "Available" balance is per category, by month, not one daily amount. v1's column was a broader "safe-to-spend view."
- **YNAB, Feature 5:** marked 🚫, but it's the closest competitor. Users can create a wish list category and set savings targets with dates, but they choose the dates and assign the money themselves. Patiencents calculates Buy Days automatically.
- **YNAB, Feature 6:** marked 🚫. Targets show progress toward savings goals, but only after users manually assign money; leftover spending money isn't automatically counted as progress.
- **PocketGuard, Feature 4:** changed from ✅ in v1 to 🚫. Its "Leftover" number is based on the month as a whole, not one daily amount. Its Pace feature tracks spending speed, but doesn't give a daily number.
- **Rule, Feature 6:** marked ✅ based on its save-to-win saving game with savings growth tracking. Confirm with its app store listing.
- **Rule, Features 2, 4, and 5:** marked 🚫 because the sources found don't mention them. Update if its app store listing shows otherwise.
- **Patiencents:** the ✅ marks describe the planned MVP, not a built product.
