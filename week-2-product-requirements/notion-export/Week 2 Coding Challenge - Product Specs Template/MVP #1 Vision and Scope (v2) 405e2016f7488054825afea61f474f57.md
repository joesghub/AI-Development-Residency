# MVP #1 Vision and Scope (v2)

> 🔄 **What changed and why (v1 → v2)**
> 
> - **New core experience: Patiencents.** v1 tested "low effort + in-the-moment impulse help + shame-free restarts." v2 tests a planning loop: Today's Number → the Patience Pot → wishlist Buy Days, with the no-backlog design as a safety net.
> - **MVP Selection moved to the top** and re-scored against the new approach.
> - **Riskiest assumption updated** to test whether users will *plan* wants instead of buying on impulse.
> - **Features replaced:** Park It, the "Should I buy this?" check, and streak-free rewards are replaced by the Patience Pot, wishlist, Buy Days, and Kept your patience.
> - **Money Kept made real:** only bank-confirmed leftovers from Today's Number count.
> - **Buckets renamed:** Bills, Essentials, and Usuals (was Bills, Needs, Wants).
> - **Platform changed:** from "iOS first" to a mobile-friendly web app, to match the required Vercel hosting. This change was noted in v1's review and is now applied.
> - **KPIs, use cases, scope, and the Build Plan handoff updated** to match.
> - **Unchanged:** the audiences and how they were identified.

> 🧪 **MVP goal:** Test our riskiest assumption: *if daily patience visibly funds the things users want, and lapses never create a backlog, adults with ADHD will plan purchases instead of buying on impulse, and keep using the app.* The MVP exists to learn, not to earn revenue.
> 

## MVP Selection

**Candidate MVPs**

| Criterion | A: Today's Number and Pot Only (no wishlist) | B: Concierge (wishlist and Buy Days managed by hand through texts) | C: Patiencents Core Loop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pain severity** (addresses the top pains P1–P4) | 2 (no planning for wants, P3) | 2 | 3 |
| **Riskiest assumption** (tests planning wants and returning after a lapse) | 1 (can't test planning) | 2 (manual help skews results) | 3 |
| **Cost and time** | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| **Learning quality** | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| **Risk** (security, technical) | 2 (bank data) | 3 | 2 (bank data) |
| **Total** | 10 | 11 | **13** |

**Selected: MVP C, the Patiencents Core Loop.**

The unique value is the *connection* between daily patience and future rewards. MVP A tests only half of it, and MVP B replaces the automation users would rely on with human help. Only MVP C tests whether the full loop changes behavior. Each feature is still kept to its minimum version: for example, Buy Days use a simple calculation, and wishlist items are added by link or by hand, with no store integrations.

**What a future MVP #2 could test:** riskier additions such as a save-from-cart browser extension, price drop alerts, or support partner sharing, before they're committed to the MMP.

# Audiences

**Audience 1: Chronic abandoners**

Adults with ADHD who have tried budgeting apps or spreadsheets, used them briefly, and quit.

- **Motivation:** They want control over their money and are frustrated that nothing has stuck. Many feel shame about it.
- **Goals:** Find a system they can keep using, stop impulse overspending, and still get the things they want.
- **Why they matter:** If Patiencents works for people who have already failed with other tools, it validates the core assumption.

**Audience 2: Newly diagnosed adults**

Adults recently diagnosed with ADHD who are learning how it affects their daily life, including money.

- **Motivation:** Their diagnosis explains past money struggles, and they're looking for tools designed for how their brain works.
- **Goals:** Understand their spending, build a simple routine, and avoid repeating past money mistakes.
- **Why they matter:** They test whether the product works without existing budgeting habits, and they're highly motivated early adopters.

**Audience 3: Support partners (deferred to MMP)**

# Problems To Solve Per Audience

*Updated to the v2 pain point framing.*

## Audience 1: Chronic abandoners

- **P1 Setup and upkeep overwhelm:** past tools required daily categorizing and reviews, so they quit.
- **P3 Impulse purchases:** they buy on impulse, then feel regret; past tools only asked them to resist or showed overspending afterward.
- **P4 Shame spiral:** returning after time away means facing a backlog and feeling like a failure.
- **P6 Abstract budgets:** monthly totals don't help with today's decisions.

## Audience 2: Newly diagnosed adults

- **P2 Out of sight, out of mind:** they have no system or clear picture of their spending.
- **P3 Impulse purchases:** they're beginning to recognize impulsivity as part of ADHD but don't have strategies yet.
- **P5 Missed bills:** time blindness and forgetfulness lead to late fees.
- **P6 Abstract budgets:** they need something concrete to plan around.

## Audience 3

Deferred to the MMP.

# Business Goals

1. **Validate the core assumption:** show that users plan wants through the wishlist and keep using the app longer than past budgeting tools.
2. **Learn which parts of the loop drive engagement:** Today's Number, the Patience Pot, or Buy Days, to guide MMP priorities.
3. **Test the reward model:** confirm that bank-confirmed leftovers feel motivating, even with a one- to two-day confirmation delay.
4. **Build an early community** of beta users who give feedback and become advocates at launch.
5. **Establish a secure foundation** for bank connections and financial data.

# Value

The MVP lets adults with ADHD:

- **Budget with almost no effort:** spending sorts automatically into Bills, Essentials, and Usuals.
- **Know what they can spend today:** one number, delivered every morning.
- **See patience pay off:** unspent money goes into the Patience Pot, confirmed by their bank.
- **Get what they want, on purpose:** wishlist items get a Buy Day when the budget can cover them.
- **Never miss a bill:** reminders before due dates.
- **Come back anytime without guilt:** missed Buy Days become "Kept your patience," and nothing piles up.

# Unique Value Proposition

Patiencents is the budgeting app that turns patience into the things you want: daily leftovers, confirmed by your bank, fill your Patience Pot and bring every wishlist item's Buy Day closer, with no backlog if you step away. *(See Product Positioning v2.)*

# Barriers to Success

| Barrier | Why It's a Risk | How We'll Address It |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ADHD abandonment patterns** | Our audience is known for dropping apps | No-backlog design; measure the comeback rate, not just daily use |
| **Wishlist adoption** | If users don't add wishlist items, the loop loses its reward | Prompt one wishlist item during onboarding; suggest ideas based on Usuals |
| **Delayed rewards** | Bank transactions take one to two days to post, so leftovers stay "pending" before they're confirmed | Show pending amounts immediately, then celebrate again on confirmation |
| **Trust and data security** | Users must link a bank account to a new app | Established bank data provider, clear security explanations, minimal data |
| **Bank connection limits and reliability** | The free Plaid Trial plan allows about 10 live connections, and syncing issues are a common competitor complaint | CSV import for additional beta testers; clear reconnect flow |
| **Matching accuracy** | Messy merchant names and combined orders can cause wrong or missed matches | Ask users to confirm medium-confidence matches; allow manual linking |
| **Irregular income** | Today's Number assumes a predictable payday | Allow manual income and payday settings; collect feedback from irregular earners |
| **Buy Days that shift** | If Buy Dates jump around, users may stop trusting them | Recalculate gently; only show changes that move a date closer, plus a weekly update otherwise |
| **Established competitors** | YNAB already markets to ADHD users | Compete on automatic Buy Days and no backlog, YNAB's weakest areas |

# Key Performance Indicators

*Targets are hypotheses to adjust once baseline data exists.*

| KPI | What It Measures | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Activation rate** | % of new users who link a bank account (or import a CSV), see Today's Number, and add one wishlist item in their first session | Tests whether setup is truly low-effort |
| **30-day and 90-day retention** | % of users still active after 30 and 90 days | Tests the core assumption |
| **Comeback rate** | % of users inactive 7+ days who return | Tests the no-backlog design |
| **Days under Today's Number** | % of days users spend less than their number | Measures steadier spending |
| **Patience Pot growth** | Average bank-confirmed leftover added per user per week | Measures real savings |
| **Wishlist adoption** | % of active users with at least one wishlist item | Tests whether users plan wants |
| **Buy Day outcomes** | Share of wishlist items bought on Buy Day, bought *before* Buy Day, or kept as patience | Buying early signals an impulse; Buy Day purchases and kept patience signal the loop working |
| **Bills paid on time** | Self-reported missed bills before vs. after | Measures real-world impact |
| **Money stress** | Check-in at signup and day 30 | Measures whether stress goes down |

## Platform Requirements

- **Mobile-friendly web app** (progressive web app) that users add to their home screen
- **Hosted on Vercel**, with code in **GitHub** and data in **Supabase**
- **Push notifications** for the morning number, Buy Days, and bill reminders
- **The core loop fits within two minutes a day**

## Data the App Must Store

| Data | Why It's Needed |
| --- | --- |
| **User accounts and profiles** | Sign-up, login, payday schedule, income, time zone, last active date |
| **Linked accounts and transactions** | Three buckets, Today's Number, and matching |
| **Bucket corrections** | User fixes, remembered by merchant |
| **Daily results** | Each day's Today's Number, Usuals spending, and leftover (pending or confirmed) |
| **Patience Pot entries** | A record of every amount added to or spent from the Pot, so Total Kept and Pot Balance are always accurate |
| **Wishlist items** | Name, price, link, merchant, reason, Buy Day, and status (waiting, bought on purpose, bought early, kept your patience) |
| **Transaction matches** | Which transaction matched which wishlist item, and the match confidence |
| **Detected bills** | Name, amount, and due date |
| **Check-in responses and events** | Money stress scores and actions used for KPIs |

## Security Requirements

*Unchanged from v1.* Secure authentication, data isolation so users only see their own data, no stored bank passwords, encryption in storage and in transit, and minimal data collection.
